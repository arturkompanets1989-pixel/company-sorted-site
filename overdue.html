What the work on each open year is made of

A late company does not start with paper. It starts with access, and with finding out how many years are open and with which office. The first four works are done once, before the first year is filed.

  1. 01

    Your filing history with both offices

    The Registrar and Inland Revenue keep it separately, and you probably cannot see either. You get the list of open periods for each office.

    Included

  2. 02

    Is the company still on the register

    The Registrar writes to a company it believes has stopped trading before it removes it. Those letters may have gone to your previous secretary. You get a written answer: on the register or not, and at what stage if letters were sent.

    Included

  3. 03

    Access to your company’s eROC account

    A late company’s account usually sits with the secretary who registered it. It is linked to your login as director by a letter to the Registrar: the Registrar’s guideline lets a director ask for the link directly. You never give us passwords.

    Included

  4. 04

    What the bill is made of, line by line

    The fee for each missing year, what the law sets for a late return, and one line for the sum the Registrar decides, with no figure next to it.

    Included

  5. 05

    The set of documents for each open year

    A written resolution instead of the missed annual meeting, the financial statements for the year, the directors’ declaration, and the annual return, Form 15, signed by a director and by the secretary. Past meetings do not have to be held now: the written resolution replaces them.

    Included, per year

  6. 06

    Filing, and a document for each year

    For every year: the filing, the receipt and the date. Work you cannot see from outside, you can see on paper.

    Included, per year

Five questions to settle before you pay

Can you lower what the Registrar charges?
No, and we say it first. The Registrar sets the sum for late filing at his own discretion. “We have connections, we will arrange it” is a common line in this market; we do not use it. What we change is that you know what the bill is made of before you pay any of it.
Who does the work and signs?
Company Sorted runs your case and the service. The accounting and secretarial work, and the signatures, come from a Sri Lankan partner firm registered to do it. The annual return is signed by a director and by the secretary, and one person cannot sign in both roles. We do not call ourselves accountants and we do not sign your filings.
Who do I pay?
World Sorted (Private) Limited, against a written invoice that lists the work. You pay the partner firm nothing. Government fees and any sum the Registrar sets are separate lines, at cost.
What can be done remotely?
Most of the catch-up. Linking the company to your login is done by a letter to the Registrar. The Registrar does not publish how long that takes, so we do not name a date. What needs your signature, and in what form, we find out before you pay.
Why trust a new company?
We do not publish invented reviews or cases. Instead: a company you can look up, World Sorted (Private) Limited, PV 00376250, a written contract, a stated list of work and a document for every year closed.
